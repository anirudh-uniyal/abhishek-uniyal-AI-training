01

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence is software that performs tasks which normally require human intelligence — understanding language, recognising patterns, making a judgement, or producing something new. That is the textbook line. The part that actually matters for your career is the shift hiding underneath it.

For fifty years, software worked one way: a human thought through every rule, and a programmer wrote those rules down. If a rule was not written, the software could not handle the situation. A payroll system knows that PF is 12% because somebody typed 12% into it. It has no idea what PF means.

AI inverts this. Instead of being given the rules, the system is given examples and works out the rules itself. Nobody can write down the rule for "this customer email sounds frustrated" — there are too many ways to be frustrated in English, let alone in Hinglish. But show a system a few million emails and it will learn the pattern.

Key idea

Traditional software is told what to do. AI is shown what good looks like and works out the rest. This is why AI can handle messy, unpredictable, human input — and also why it is never 100% certain.

Narrow AI vs General AI

Every AI system in commercial use today is Narrow AI — extremely capable at a defined range of tasks, and completely blank outside it. A model that writes excellent sales emails has no awareness that it is doing sales, no goals of its own, and no memory of you between conversations unless it has been specifically built to keep one.

General AI — a system with human-level ability across any domain, with its own understanding and intent — does not exist. It is a research ambition, not a product you can buy. Treat any vendor claim that sounds like general AI as marketing.

Why AI became useful now

AI as a field is from the 1950s. Three things changed in the last decade and turned it from a lab subject into something your employer expects you to use:

  • Data. Digitisation gave us the enormous text and image collections these systems learn from.
  • Compute. GPUs made it economically possible to train on that data.
  • Architecture. The transformer design (2017) let models handle long stretches of language with real context, not just word-by-word.
  • Access. From 2022, chat interfaces removed the need to be a programmer. A business analyst could now use a frontier model directly.
Career framing

AI is not a product you buy — it is a capability you insert into a process. Companies are not hiring people who can name AI tools. They are hiring people who can look at an existing business process, find the step that is slow because a human is reading or writing something, and redesign that step. Everything in this program trains that skill.

02

AI vs Machine Learning vs Deep Learning

These three terms get used as if they mean the same thing. They do not. They sit inside one another, and being able to place them correctly is a standard interview question.

Each term sits inside the one before it. Generative AI is a specific use of deep learning, which is a specific kind of machine learning, which is one route to AI.

TermWhat it isHow it worksBusiness example
Artificial Intelligence The overall goal: machines performing tasks that need intelligence Any technique at all — including hand-written rules A chatbot that routes a customer to the right department
Machine Learning A method of achieving AI by learning from data instead of being programmed Humans choose which features matter; the model learns the weights Predicting which leads are likely to convert, from past sales data
Deep Learning Machine learning using neural networks with many layers The model discovers the features itself — no human feature engineering Reading a scanned purchase order and extracting the line items
Generative AI Deep learning models that produce new content Predicts the most probable next piece of content, over and over Drafting a proposal, summarising a meeting, writing a job description

The distinction that actually matters

Machine learning needs a human to decide what the model should look at. To predict lead quality, someone must decide that company size, industry and budget are the useful signals. Deep learning removes that step — given enough examples, the network works out for itself which signals matter, including combinations no human would have thought to specify.

That is why deep learning took over anything involving language, images or audio: nobody can list the features that make a sentence sound angry, but a network trained on enough sentences can find them.

Interview answer

"AI is the objective. Machine learning is the main way we get there — the system learns patterns from data rather than following written rules. Deep learning is machine learning with multi-layer neural networks, which is what made language and images work. Generative AI is deep learning applied to creating new content."

03

What is Generative AI?

Until about 2020, almost all commercial AI was predictive. You gave it something that already existed and it told you something about it: is this transaction fraudulent, will this customer churn, is this resume a match, what is this photo of. Useful, but the output was always a label, a score or a number.

Generative AI creates something that did not exist before. You describe what you need and it produces the artefact: the email, the summary, the analysis, the code, the image, the structured record.

Predictive AIGenerative AI
Question it answers"What is this?" / "What will happen?""Make me this."
OutputA category, score or numberNew content — text, image, code, audio
ExampleThis lead scores 82/100Here is the follow-up email for this lead
Who used itData teamsEveryone in the business

Why this changed everything for business roles

Look at what a marketing executive, an HR generalist, a sales manager or a business analyst actually produces in a day. Emails. Job descriptions. Campaign copy. Meeting notes. Proposals. Comparison sheets. Reports. Almost all professional output is content — and content is exactly what generative AI makes.

Predictive AI made data scientists more productive. Generative AI makes everyone else more productive. That is why your employer now expects fluency in it regardless of your function, and it is the reason this program exists.

Key idea

Generative AI does not remove the need for judgement — it removes the cost of the first draft. The value you add moves from producing the document to deciding whether the document is right.

04

LLMs — how a ChatGPT-type system actually works

LLM stands for Large Language Model. You do not need the mathematics, but you do need an accurate mental model — because almost every mistake people make with AI at work comes from imagining the wrong one.

It predicts the next piece of text. That is all.

An LLM breaks language into tokens — roughly, word fragments. "Understanding" might be two tokens; "ERP" might be one. Given the tokens so far, the model calculates which token is most likely to come next, adds it, and repeats. A 400-word email is that same prediction running several hundred times.

The reason this produces something coherent rather than gibberish is scale. Trained on an enormous amount of human writing, "predict what comes next" forces the model to internalise grammar, tone, structure, domain conventions and a great deal of factual association — because all of those things shape what word genuinely comes next.

The three stages of training

1 · Pre-trainingReads a vast body of text and learns language, structure and world patterns. Expensive, done once.
→
2 · Fine-tuningTrained on example instructions and good answers, so it responds to requests instead of just continuing text.
→
3 · AlignmentHumans rate competing answers; the model is tuned toward the helpful, safe, well-formatted ones.

Stage 3 is why the model sounds polite and confident. Note carefully: it was rewarded for answers people rated highly. Confidence was rewarded. Accuracy was rewarded only when the rater happened to know the truth. Hold on to that — it explains Section 07.

The context window is its only memory

Everything the model can "see" at once — your instruction, the document you pasted, the conversation so far — sits in a fixed-size context window. Inside the window, it has perfect access. Outside, the information does not exist for it.

Three practical consequences:

  • It knows nothing about your company unless you put it in the window. It has never seen your price list, your policies or your customers.
  • Very long documents must be broken up, or the model will work with only part of them.
  • A new chat starts blank. Context does not carry over unless the product is explicitly built to store it.

The same question can give different answers

The model picks from a probability distribution rather than always taking the single most likely token, which is what keeps writing natural instead of robotic. The side effect is that identical prompts can produce different output. This is normal and expected — but it means an LLM must never sit in a process that requires an identical result every single time.

The most important sentence in this module

An LLM generates text that is statistically plausible. It is not looking anything up, and it has no internal check for truth. Fluent and correct feel identical coming out of it. Verification is your job, not the model's.

05

Generative AI vs traditional software

Both are software, and that is where the similarity ends. Confusing the two is the most expensive mistake a business makes with AI, so this comparison is worth learning properly.

Traditional softwareGenerative AI
LogicRules written by a humanPatterns learned from data
Same input twiceIdentical output, alwaysMay differ slightly each time
AccuracyExactApproximate, usually very good
Unexpected inputRejects it or throws an errorAttempts an answer anyway
How it failsLoudly — it crashes or refusesQuietly — a confident wrong answer
Handles ambiguityNoYes, this is its strength
TestingPass / failJudged on quality, needs sampling and review
Change a behaviourEdit the code, redeployChange the instruction, immediate

Row five is the one that costs companies money. When a traditional system fails you know instantly — a screen errors, a job aborts, someone raises a ticket. When an LLM fails, you get a well-written paragraph with a wrong number in it, and it looks exactly like the paragraphs that were right.

A rule you can apply on day one

Use traditional software

When the answer must be exactly right, every time

  1. Salary and PF calculation
  2. GST and tax computation
  3. Accounting entries and ledgers
  4. Inventory quantity on hand
  5. Invoice totals and payments
  6. Anything with a legal or audit consequence
Use generative AI

When the task involves language, judgement or variety

  1. Summarising a long email thread
  2. Drafting proposals and outreach
  3. Categorising unstructured enquiries
  4. Extracting fields from messy text
  5. Explaining what a report implies
  6. Generating options to choose between
Key idea

The strongest business systems use both. AI reads the unstructured, human side of the problem — the email, the resume, the complaint — and converts it into clean structured data. Traditional software then does the exact, auditable work with that data. Module 8 builds this pattern properly with ERP and CRM.

06

AI capabilities and limitations

Professional competence with AI is mostly knowing where the boundary is. People who underestimate it waste hours on work the model would have done in seconds. People who overestimate it send a customer something wrong.

What it is genuinely excellent at

  • Summarising — a 30-page report or a 60-message thread into what matters.
  • Drafting — the first 80% of any document, in seconds instead of an hour.
  • Rewriting — changing tone, length, language or audience while keeping meaning.
  • Structuring the unstructured — turning a rambling email into clean fields. This is the single highest-value business use.
  • Classifying — sorting enquiries, tickets or feedback into categories at volume.
  • Explaining — making a technical or financial concept clear for a specific audience.
  • Generating options — twelve subject lines, six objection responses, five campaign angles.

Where it is weak or unsafe

LimitationWhat goes wrongHow you work around it
No live knowledgeTraining has a cut-off date; recent events are unknown or guessedUse a tool with search, or paste the current information in yourself
No company knowledgeIt invents your pricing, policies and product detailsAlways supply the real document in the prompt
Arithmetic at scaleMulti-step calculations drift; totals look right but are notLet it set up the logic; compute in Excel and verify
Not a source of truthStatistics, citations and case law can be fabricatedVerify every figure against a real source before use
No accountabilityThe model cannot own a decision or its consequencesA named human signs off on anything that leaves the company
Confidentiality riskData pasted into a public tool may leave your controlFollow company policy; never paste customer or salary data into public tools
The 80% principle

Treat AI output as a strong first draft from a fast, well-read intern who has never met your customers and will not admit uncertainty. The last 20% — the facts, the numbers, the relationship, the judgement — is where you earn your salary.

07

Hallucination, bias and inaccurate information

This is the section that separates someone who is employable with AI from someone who is a liability with it.

Hallucination

A hallucination is output that is fluent, confident, well-formatted and factually false. It is not a bug that will eventually be patched away — it follows directly from how the technology works. The model produces the most plausible continuation. When it does not have the fact, the most plausible continuation is a fact-shaped sentence, so it writes one.

What it typically looks like in a workplace:

  • Invented figures — "the Indian ERP market grew 23.4% in 2024", stated with no source and no basis.
  • Fake references — a plausible report title from a real consulting firm that was never published.
  • Fabricated product detail — features, modules or integrations your product does not have.
  • Wrong attribution — a real quotation assigned to the wrong person or company.
  • Confident policy — a clause of law or a compliance rule stated crisply and incorrectly.
Why this is a business risk, not a technical curiosity

A hallucinated number in a client proposal is a credibility loss. A hallucinated compliance statement is a legal exposure. A hallucinated specification in a tender is a commitment your company may have to honour. Lawyers in several countries have been sanctioned for filing AI-generated citations that did not exist.

Bias

The model learned from human-written text, so it absorbed human patterns — including the unfair ones. Bias shows up as systematically different treatment of similar people or cases:

  • Resume screening that quietly favours certain colleges, cities, names or career paths.
  • Job descriptions written in language that skews the applicant pool.
  • Performance summaries that describe identical behaviour differently by gender.
  • Default assumptions — that a "software engineer" is male, that a good candidate studied in a metro.

Bias is harder to catch than hallucination because each individual output looks reasonable. You only see it in the aggregate — which is exactly why an AI-assisted process needs auditing, not just spot-checking. Module 5 takes this further in a recruitment setting.

Your verification playbook

PracticeWhat it means in daily work
Ground itPaste the real source document instead of relying on the model's memory. A model working from your text hallucinates far less than one working from recall.
Check every numberTreat all figures, dates, percentages and names as unverified until you have seen them in a real source.
Demand checkable sourcesAsk for references, then open them. An unopened citation is worth nothing.
Ask it to challenge itself"What in this answer are you least confident about, and what would make it wrong?" surfaces weak claims quickly.
Scale scrutiny to stakesAn internal brainstorm needs a glance. A customer proposal, an offer letter or anything with a rupee figure needs line-by-line checking.
Protect confidential dataNo salary data, customer records, contracts or unreleased plans in public tools.
Keep a human accountableEvery AI-assisted output that leaves the company has a named person who owns it.
What this looks like to an employer

In an interview, "AI sometimes makes mistakes" is a weak answer. "AI hallucinates because it predicts plausible text rather than retrieving facts, so I ground it in source documents and verify every figure before anything goes to a client" is the answer that gets you hired.

08

Human + AI collaboration

The useful question is not "will AI replace this job". It is "which parts of this job should AI do, and which parts must stay human". Organisations that get this split right see real gains. Organisations that get it wrong either ban AI entirely or let it write to customers unchecked — both are expensive.

Give to AIKeep with the human
Reading 500 documents nobody has time to readDeciding what the organisation does about them
The first draft of anythingThe final wording that carries your name
Converting messy text into structured fieldsConfirming the extraction is actually correct
Generating options and anglesChoosing, based on the relationship and the context
Consistent formatting and repetitive rewritingEthics, fairness and the judgement calls
Speed and volumeAccountability for the outcome

The working loop

1 · FrameDefine the task, the audience, the constraints and the output format you need.
→
2 · GenerateLet AI produce the draft, the extraction or the set of options.
→
3 · VerifyCheck facts, figures, tone and fit. Correct or regenerate what fails.
→
4 · DecideApply judgement, take the decision, own the result.

Notice that steps 1, 3 and 4 are entirely human, and they are where all the professional skill sits. Someone who only knows step 2 has learned a tool. Someone who is strong at 1, 3 and 4 has learned a way of working — and that is what the rest of this program develops.

The honest version

AI will not replace you. But a colleague who has restructured their work around AI will produce three times what you produce, at similar quality — and that comparison is what actually affects careers. The goal of this program is to make sure you are that colleague.

Real-world case

500 customer enquiries every month

A mid-sized technology company receives roughly 500 enquiries a month through its website, email and trade-show contacts. They arrive as free text — some detailed, some a single line, some not sales enquiries at all.

Traditional approach

One executive, manually

  1. Opens and reads every enquiry
  2. Works out what the customer wants
  3. Categorises it by hand
  4. Assigns it to a salesperson
  5. Types a short summary — when there is time
AI-assisted approach

AI first pass, human review

  1. AI reads the enquiry
  2. Identifies the actual requirement
  3. Scores it Hot / Warm / Cold
  4. Extracts company, budget, requirement, timeline
  5. Writes a briefing for the salesperson
500enquiries per month
~6 minmanual handling, each
50 hrsper month, manual
~8 hrswith AI, review only

Indicative figures for discussion. The point is not the exact saving — it is that the executive moves from processing enquiries to acting on the ones that matter.

The real benefit is not the time saved

When a human handles 500 enquiries manually, the last hundred get less attention than the first. Categorisation drifts with mood and workload. Enquiries that arrive on a busy day sit unread. Good leads go cold silently — and nobody can see it happening.

An AI-assisted pass changes three things beyond speed:

  • Consistency. Enquiry 500 is assessed by the same standard as enquiry 1.
  • Structure. Free text becomes fields, so the pipeline becomes measurable and reportable.
  • Speed of response. Hot leads surface within minutes rather than days, and response time is the strongest predictor of B2B conversion.
Where the human stays

AI proposes the category and drafts the summary. A salesperson still checks the Hot leads before acting, because a misread budget or a missed nuance is a lost deal. The AI is doing the reading, not the selling.

Practical activity

Triage ten live enquiries

Below are ten enquiries of the kind the company in the case study actually receives. Work through them with an AI tool. Not all of them are sales leads — noticing that is part of the exercise.

#Enquiry as received
1"We are a manufacturing unit in Pune, around 120 staff. Current system is Tally and it is not handling production planning. Need ERP with production and quality modules. Budget approx 8–10 lakh. Want to go live before the next financial year."
2"Sir please send me the price list."
3"I am a final year B.Tech student. Do you offer internships in your development team?"
4"Real estate developer, Nashik. Sales team of 25 people, currently on spreadsheets. Looking at CRM. We are evaluating three vendors and will decide within three weeks. Can you do a demo on Thursday?"
5"Our GST report is showing wrong figures for last month. This is urgent, our filing is due. Ticket number GT-4412."
6"IT services company, about 400 employees. Thinking about an HRMS at some point, probably next financial year. Just want to understand what is available. No budget approved yet."
7"Pharma distribution, Mumbai. We need batch-wise inventory with expiry tracking — we are losing stock to expiry every quarter. Around 15 lakh approved. Need this running in 6 weeks."
8"Textile SME in Surat. We implemented an ERP two years ago, it never worked properly and we want to migrate. 60 users. Budget around 12 lakh, planning for Q3."
9"Hi, I have 6 years experience in ERP implementation. Attaching my CV, please consider me for any suitable opening."
10"Auto component supplier, Chakan. Need ERP with quality module for our IATF audit. Audit is in four months. Please send a proposal and arrange a demo next week."

Your four tasks

  1. Categorise each enquiry. Sales lead, support request, careers, or not relevant. Three of the ten are not sales leads — find them.
  2. Extract the requirement. For every genuine lead, pull out company type, location, product needed, user count, budget and timeline. Mark anything that was not stated as "not stated" — do not let the AI fill the gap with an invented value.
  3. Assign a priority. Hot, Warm or Cold. Write the reason in one line. Priority should reflect budget clarity, timeline urgency and how specific the requirement is.
  4. Write the sales summary. For the Hot leads, produce a three-line briefing a salesperson could read thirty seconds before calling: what they need, why now, and what to open the conversation with.

Deliver it as a table

Ask the AI for a single table with these columns — structured output is far more useful than prose, and this is the habit Module 2 builds on:

The trap to watch for

Enquiry 2 gives the AI almost nothing. Many models will still confidently produce a company size and an industry. Check your output for exactly this — the moment you catch the model inventing a field, Section 07 has stopped being theory.

How this is assessed

Not on speed. You are assessed on whether you caught the three non-leads, whether your priority reasoning is defensible to a sales manager, and whether you spotted anything the AI invented. Bring your corrections to the discussion — a clean catch is worth more than a fast table.

Where this applies

This single workflow — read unstructured input, classify it, extract fields, summarise for a human — sits underneath a large share of business AI. You will meet it again in Sales and CRM as lead qualification, in Customer Support as ticket triage, in HR as resume screening, and in Operations as purchase-order processing. Learn it properly here and the later modules become variations on a pattern you already know.
